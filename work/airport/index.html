Paying what was never paid

Lima Airport had never charged international transfer passengers a fee. I designed and tested the web prototype to collect it.

Airport Operations Customer Experience UX Research UX Design Service Design
Roles — Innovation Specialist · UX/UI designer · CX designer Duration — May – Oct 2025 · Lima Airport Partners Scope — Planning → UX/UI design → prototyping → evaluation → handoff
Open the live TUUA platform

As Innovation specialist, I designed the TUUA Transfer web payment flow to solve the lack of a fee collection channel for connecting passengers, working with the airport's central departments. Today, the implemented system successfully collects this fee from over 130,000 international transfer passengers each month.

Act 00 · The Context

One fee between plane changes.

How a payment affects the experience during a connecting flight.

The passenger journey

  1. Arrive on international flight
  2. Pay Transfer feeThe missing channel
  3. Pass eGate and turnstile
  4. Board next international flight

A physical-digital model. The fee is paid inside the airport, mid-connection. Three staffed points carry most payments, issuing a bar-code sticker for the boarding pass. A web channel, exclusive to this fee, lets passengers skip peak-hour queues with a QR. Both end at the eGate.

Act 01 · The Problem

A fee with no way to collect it.

Five moments from concession to collection, and the income at stake.

From concession to collection, 2001–2025
  1. 1Feb 2001The concessionLAP begins operating Jorge Chávez under concession with Peruvian State.
  2. 2Mar 2013An income promiseAmendment N°6 allows transfer fees upon delivery of new terminal.
  3. 3Jun 2025The terminal opensThe fee becomes chargeable, but no mechanism exists to collect it.
  4. 4Aug 2025Commercial termsLAP and Airlines negotiated fee collection inside the ticket.
  5. 5Oct 2025Mechanism readyImplementation of fee mechanism at the airport, led by LAP Finance.

The fee at today's volume

130,000+Intl. transfer passengers / monthWould be charged today.
≈ $43,600Fee a day, before taxPreviously left uncollected.
≈ $23,300For Lima Airport, a dayThe Peruvian State takes 46.5% as royalty.

130,000 × $10.05 ÷ 30 days. $10.05 is the fee before 18% IGV, which makes it $11.86. Lima Airport keeps 53.489%; the State takes 46.511% as royalty under the concession. An estimate at today's volume, not a measured loss.

Act 03 · Scope & Deliverables

Five artifacts for production.

Step through the deliverables I produced.

  1. Technical scopes

    01 · Platform scope

    Passenger side: data capture, card payment, and an e-receipt with an eGate QR. Back office: sales reports reconciled with gateway and real-time sync with airport's fee system, sized for 4 million+ passengers a year.

  2. Technical scopes

    02 · Payment gateway scope

    E-payments confirmed in real time, returning the data for e-receipt and access QR. PCI DSS certified, 99.9% availability and peaks of 1,500 transactions an hour, with daily reconciliation and chargeback reports.

  3. Experience

    03 · Screen mockup and web prototype

    Four mobile screens: personal data, email code, card payment and the QR ticket for the eGate. A clickable mockup of the full flow was used in the web review with stakeholders.

    See the screens

  4. Operations

    04 · Operational casuistry

    Three models of e-payment in transport put initial web adoption at 5–7% of int-int passengers. Demand was split across web, cash desks, eGate POS and mobile POS, so PlanOps could size counters and queues.

  5. Operations

    05 · Process map

    A service blueprint of the payment experience, differentiating tasks performed by the passenger, front office, back office and automation.

Disclosure notes

My participation. I understand the platform's mechanism and contributed to its UX/UI design — not its development, deployment or operation. Work testimony available upon formal request.

What's next: a lower fee, inside the ticket. In July 2026, LAP proposed cutting the fee from $10.05 to $6.40 + IGV and moving it into the ticket. Airlines haven't agreed; collection in the terminal continues.

My stance on the fee. Designing its collection channel doesn't make me a party to the fee itself. Whether it continues is for Peru's Judiciary to decide: in June 2026, a Lima court allowed a constitutional challenge to proceed, while collection continues.
