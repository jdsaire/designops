Charging what was never charged

Lima Airport had never charged international transfer passengers a fee. I designed and tested the web prototype to collect it.

Airport Operations Customer Experience UX Research UX Design Service Design
Roles — Innovation Specialist · UX/UI designer · CX designer Duration — May – Oct 2025 · Lima Airport Partners Scope — Planning → UX/UI design → prototyping → evaluation → handoff
Open the live TUUA platform

As Innovation specialist, I designed the TUUA Transfer web payment flow to solve the lack of a fee collection channel for connecting passengers, working with the airport's central departments. Today, the implemented system successfully collects this fee from over 130,000 international transfer passengers each month.

Act 00 · The Context

An airport fee with no benchmark.

Follow the int-int transfer journey, then compare the ways to pay.

The connection, step by step

  1. Arrive on international flight
  2. Pay Transfer feeThe missing channel
  3. Pass eGate and turnstile
  4. Board next international flight

A physical-digital model. The fee is collected inside the airport, during the international connection. Three staffed points carry most payments; a web channel, built only for this fee, lets passengers skip the queues at peak hours. Both end at the same eGate.

Act 06 · The Contrast

The platform is live, it isn't mine, and the gap is public.

A year later, I reviewed the live platform against my proposal. Select any step to compare the two.

Public, unauthenticated pages only. No transaction was submitted, no session was captured, and no data was collected beyond what any visitor sees.

My proposal
Live today

Reordered, not removed. Personal-data capture moved from my first screen to the live flow's fourth, after the flight lookup. I read the reordering as intuitive and do not contest it.

No counterpart before payment. None of the four public screens asks for an email code.

$11.86, a screen earlier. The live fee appears on the flight-details screen, a full screen earlier than I proposed, and matches in both places it appears. Fee-setting was never my decision.

Beyond the boundary. The review stopped before payment, so the live ticket was not observed.

An entry page first. The live flow opens on a homepage that explains the fee before the first step.

A click instead of a check. Eligibility is a self-declaration, "Soy ese tipo de pasajero", where the technical scope specified an automatic agreement check. To my own knowledge of the build, that check was never implemented as written; the review could not see past its boundary to confirm it.

Where the review stops. Public, unauthenticated pages only: one control short of paying, with no transaction submitted.
