Act 00 · The Context

AI disrupts work. Careers can't adapt.

Three shifts that make a proven personal brand urgent in 2026.

The shift

A fifth of jobs in motion

By 2030, 22% of today's jobs will be disrupted: 170 million created, 92 million displaced, and 39% of core skills changing.

The senior gap

Experience, filtered out

For AI roles, 90% of US hiring managers would consider candidates under 35, but only 32% would consider anyone over 60, whatever their record.

The edge

Proof beats experience

66% of leaders would not hire someone without AI skills, and 71% would choose a less experienced candidate who has them.

Sources: World Economic Forum, Future of Jobs Report 2025; Generation, Age-Proofing AI, October 2024; Microsoft and LinkedIn, Work Trend Index 2024.

Act 01 · The Problem

Credentials open doors. They don't prove building capabilities.

Three reasons a strong résumé is no longer enough.

The reading

Posts list requirements, not voices

Job posts spell out what a candidate must have, never how they think. Meeting every requirement gets an application read; being understood gets it believed.

The credential

Diplomas don't show the build

A work sample predicts performance 3× better than education, and none of my flagship credentials is one. This live page translates those honors into proof.

The claim

Fluent is free now

Three in four hiring teams report AI-written applications, and 65% of hiring managers find skills harder to verify. Only a verifiable build stands out.

Sources: Sackett et al. (2022) on selection validity; Robert Half, November 2025, n = 2,000+; Deel Global Hiring Report 2026.

From renting a template to directing every line.

The Template era (2023–25)

My first portfolios ran on Canva, Figma and Framer. None matched the fidelity my UX authority demanded, and coding meant restarting as a junior.

Level 2

The Single-Page (Q2 2026)

Not a prompt to brag about: weeks on one scroll gave me the flight hours no university could, locking the AI workflows behind this site.

Level 3

The Multi-Page (Now)

The version you are reading: dedicated About and Contact pages, four copy-tested case briefs, my most capable front-end to date, and the Microsoft specialization finished.

Building a site with AI in 2027 takes more than a prompt: it takes a whole orchestration, from research to review. That is what sets this build apart from audacious vibe-coders and from developers who still write every line by hand.

Act 04 · The Architecture

Four choices keep this site scalable.

The technical stack across the page, in plain words.

Speed

1) Light, fast and built to last

The site runs on the web's three core languages, with no framework so pages load fast and nothing breaks after a hard change.

Vanilla HTML, CSS and JavaScript: the web's native standards, with no framework, no library and no build step, deployed as static files on GitHub Pages. Every file runs as written and reads end to end. No major-version migrations, no dependency to patch, full control of every byte, and a light payload that loads fast on an average phone.

Accessibility

2) Made for everyone

Anyone can navigate with a keyboard or a screen reader, with text readable in light and dark themes, on a phone or a wide screen.

Built to WCAG AA: every meaningful image carries alt text, every control is reachable by keyboard with a visible focus state, and contrast is verified in both themes rather than assumed. Layout resolves at three declared breakpoints — 767px and below, 768 to 1023, and 1024 up.

Copy

3) Every word in its place

Every word lives in one of three places: text every page shares, text every case brief shares, and text for one page only.

Internationalization (i18n) runs on three layers of JSON dictionaries, merged in order: global for site chrome, regional for the strings every brief shares, local for each brief's own copy. Every string sits under one key at one level, and each English locale file has a Spanish twin with the same keys, checked by script. Spanish is transcreated, not translated, and fallbacks always read the approved English.

Design System

4) The same templates on every brief

Every case brief uses the same interface components as templates: acts, cards, panels, charts and dashboards.

Atomic design run as DesignOps. Global tokens for colour, type and spacing feed atoms such as buttons and tags, molecules such as cards and reveal panels, and organisms such as this schedule and dashboard. Each brief builds the same organisms from its own data and dictionary, so one fix lands everywhere, and every change is checked at three widths, two themes and two languages.

Act 06 · The Reflection

What this web pipeline taught.

Three lessons: governance, tracking and audience.

Governance

Stops that hold

A run stops at every gate until I release it. Review happens on the running page before changes are effective in live build.

Tracking

Decide once

Every decision becomes a numbered ruling in one log, and every edit I make is recorded without a project manager.

Audience

Say it clearly

Every claim was traced to its source, and optimized to be understood by professionals outside the Systems Engineering world.
